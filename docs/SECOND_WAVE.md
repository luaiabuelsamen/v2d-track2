# Second wave: why the weak clips are weak, and the cheapest thing to try next

The 30-clip combined run scored **mean kit AUC 0.3908** (`results/t2_combined_run_record.json`).
Twelve clips came in under 0.30 and carry the whole deficit: their mean is **0.1808** against
0.5308 for the other eighteen. This triages those twelve from their own rollouts, proposes one
change per failure mode, and prices each at $3 per clip-run (the ledger rate: $88.22 for 30 clips).

Nothing here has been launched. Budget position: **$141.78 left** of the $230.

Definitions, all computed with the registered rule's own constants so the buckets and the selection
cannot disagree:

- **lost mid-clip** — never falls below z = 0.15 m, but passes 10 cm from the reference and stays
  past it to the end. 10 cm is the evaluator's own object position gate.
- **fell back** — not one of the seven checkpoints was drop-free, so `pick-checkpoint` took the
  longest hold. The policy never kept the object for a whole clip at any point in training.
- **held, drifting** — inside 10 cm for every frame. The AUC is lost to pose, not to loss.
- **never locks on** — past the gate for more than a quarter of the clip but never sustained: it
  loses the object and recovers, over and over. Only e01.
- **dropped** — *empty*, and that is a property of the selection rule, not of the policies: z-drop
  rejects dropping checkpoints wherever a drop-free one exists.

### Two findings that constrain everything below

**1. Nothing goes wrong because the reference jumped.** At each failure frame the reference is
moving 0.12–1.67 cm/frame, against per-clip maxima of 0.81–5.95 cm/frame elsewhere in the same
clip. The reference is at its calmest when the policy loses the object. These are policy failures.

**2. Five clips already beat their own reference's score** — e12 +0.116, e00 +0.110, e18 +0.041,
e08 +0.009, e21 +0.002 over the ceiling. A physics rollout can land closer to Tier 1 truth than the
noisy reference it tracked, because contact and support constrain it. So "ceiling" is a marker, not
a wall; but a clip sitting *at* it has no cheap headroom left. (Caveat: the ceiling file scores on
the sample grid.)

## One proposed change per bucket

### Bucket 1 — lost mid-clip (6 clips): drop the START patch and re-measure

**The change.** Revert `immediate_first_frame_probability` to the stock curriculum and
`reset.reset_voc_scale` to the stock value, keeping everything else. One variable.

**Evidence.** The START patch pushes late-stage resets to the first frame (0.25 from 209.9M, 0.5
from 288.7M) and the runner pins `reset.reset_voc_scale=0.0`, so in late training the policy
almost only ever starts at frame 0. A clip that loses the object at frame 118 of 992 then collects
no late-clip experience at all for the rest of training: the states it fails in are the states it
stops visiting. That matches the bucket exactly — these six track the object to within 1.9–6.6 cm,
lose it at one frame, and the reference is smooth there. It is also the one arm in the shipping
recipe that was never isolated, and the cheapest to un-change.

**Why not a reward change for this bucket.** Their orientation error while holding is 4.3–47.7°
and their position error 1.9–6.6 cm; they are tracking well right up to the loss. The failure is
where training looked, not what it was rewarded for.

**Cost.** 6 clips × $3 = **$18**, one seed. Attributable against the per-clip record we hold.

### Bucket 2 — fell back (3 clips): widen `object_orientation_std` from 0.3 to 0.8 rad

**The change.** `--std 0.8` on our existing patch (`brev/patch_object_orientation.py` already takes
it). One variable.

**Evidence.** The term is `exp(-(θ/std)²)`. At std = 0.3 rad it is numerically dead exactly where
these clips live:

| θ | std 0.3 | std 0.5 | std 0.8 |
|---|---|---|---|
| 24.1° | 1.4e-01 | 4.9e-01 | 7.6e-01 |
| 48.4° (e17) | **3.6e-04** | 5.8e-02 | 3.3e-01 |
| 51.9° (e04) | **1.1e-04** | 3.8e-02 | 2.8e-01 |
| 82.3° (e25) | **1.1e-10** | 2.6e-04 | 4.0e-02 |
| 91.3° (e07) | **5.6e-13** | 3.9e-05 | 1.9e-02 |

The orientation reward we added and paid for contributes 1e-4 to 1e-13 of its maximum on the
clips with the worst orientation — no value and no gradient. This bucket's mean angle while holding
is 60.9°. Widening the std is the one change that makes the term we already shipped do anything
here, and these three also have the largest ceiling gap of any bucket (0.715 against 0.176).

**Cost.** 3 clips × $3 = **$9**. Run it on e03 e09 e18 e24 as well if you want it judged against
the four-clip record: 7 clips, **$21**.

### Bucket 3 — held, drifting (2 clips): change nothing

**The change.** None. Do not spend on e13 or e21.

**Evidence.** e21 scores 0.206 against a reference ceiling of 0.204 and e13 0.159 against 0.193.
Combined headroom to their own references is +0.03 across both clips, or +0.002 on the 30-clip
mean. They hold the object inside 10 cm for every frame; there is no tracking failure to fix. The
only lever is a better reference, which is a cleaner question, not a policy one. The oracle gain
here (+0.053) is also almost entirely e13's +0.106, which is checkpoint noise, not a method.

**Cost.** **$0**, and it protects $6 from being spent on nothing.

### Bucket 4 — never locks on (1 clip): train e01 on the smoothed reference after all

**The change.** Re-run e01 against the bridge-smoothed reference instead of the raw one.

**Evidence.** e01 is the one clip flagged `fall_back_to_raw`: the smoother lowered its *input
ceiling* (0.380 → 0.350), so staging kept the raw Tier 2 pose. But input ceiling is a scoring
criterion, not a trainability one, and e01 is the only clip in the run whose policy never settles —
past the 10 cm gate on 63% of frames, recovering each time, against a reference whose frame-to-frame
step reaches 3.35 cm. A policy chasing an unsmoothed pose is a different problem from a scorer
measuring one. This is the cheapest test of that distinction we will ever get, and the decision it
revisits was made on the wrong axis.

**Cost.** 1 clip × $3 = **$3**.

## The second-seed option

**The noise floor is measurable and it is large.** Across all 30 clips, the standard deviation of
kit AUC over each clip's last five checkpoints is **0.0756 on average and 0.2315 at worst**. Those
checkpoints are 50M steps apart in one run, so this is a lower bound on run-to-run spread, not an
upper one. The one direct seed measurement we have agrees: at 10M steps on e24, stock arm A's two
seeds differed by 0.0003, but arm C (a changed objective) differed by **0.158**
(`results/b3_arms.json`).

**What that buys and costs.**

| option | cost | what it resolves |
|---|---|---|
| no second seed | $0 | a per-clip result carries ±0.076; a 6-clip arm's mean carries ±0.031 |
| second seed on the winning arm only | $18–21 | halves the error on the arm that matters |
| second seed on all 12 weak clips | $36 | spread on the whole weak set |
| full second seed, 30 clips | $90 | a submittable second entry as well as the spread |

**Recommendation: do not buy a blanket second seed.** Run the four proposals at one seed for $30,
then buy a second seed only for whichever arm wins, on the clips it targets. A +0.05 effect on a
6-clip arm is 1.6σ at one seed and 2.3σ at two, so the second seed is worth buying *after* an arm
looks like it won and not before. The exception is if a second full 30-clip entry is wanted for its
own sake, which is a submission decision, not an experimental one.

**What the first wave does not need.** The four-clip bar result (0.36635 → 0.59932) is +0.233 on a
four-clip mean whose one-seed standard error is 0.038. It is six sigma clear and needs no second
seed to stand.

## Selection rules at 30 clips — information only, the rule is unchanged

Recomputed over all 30 clips (`results/selection_rule_check.json`):

| rule | mean kit AUC | reads truth |
|---|---|---|
| **z_drop_registered** (submitted) | **0.3908** | no |
| latest_drop_free | 0.3946 | no |
| final_checkpoint | 0.3898 | no |
| oracle_reads_truth | 0.4201 | **yes — upper bound, unsubmittable** |

**This corrects something stated earlier in the run.** On the first eight clips z-drop trailed the
alternatives, and on seeing six weak clips pick a 49M–149M checkpoint I said the registered rule
was "costing us real points on the tail". At 30 clips that is wrong. The three legal rules sit
inside 0.005 of each other, and the oracle — which reads Tier 1 truth and so is the ceiling on *any*
selection rule — is only **+0.029** above the registered one. Checkpoint selection cannot be worth
more than 0.029 mean AUC, and no submittable rule can collect even that. The 0.21 of headroom to
the reference ceiling is a policy problem, which is why every proposal above is a policy or
reference change and none of them touches selection.

z-drop stays the registered submission rule. Switching to `latest_drop_free` for +0.0038 would
trade a pre-registered rule for noise a quarter the size of a single clip's checkpoint spread.
